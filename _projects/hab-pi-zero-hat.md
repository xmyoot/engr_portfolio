This is a 65 × 30 mm interface board that plugs onto the 40-pin header of a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W. It replaces hand-soldered wiring in a high-altitude balloon flight computer with labelled, locking connectors.

- **What it carries:** the Pi's 5 V input, the I2C, UART and SPI buses, and switched power for an SPI camera.
- **Where it flies:** in an insulated foam capsule, so it was designed for cold, vibration and a parachute landing.
- **How it's built:** the design is two layers, and the fab assembles it fully (SMT plus through-hole) with a conformal coating.

# Board
The board has connectors on three edges, all mating outward, so every cable can be plugged in with the HAT installed. The 2×20 socket sits on the underside, aligned pad for pad with the Pi's header.

# Schematic
**5 V input path.** The 5 V input goes through an LM74700-Q1 ideal-diode controller driving an N-channel MOSFET. The bulk capacitance sits on the header side.

![5 V input and ideal diode schematic](/assets/images/hab-hat-sch-power.png)

**Camera power and SPI.** The camera rail comes from a TPS22918 load switch with controlled rise time and quick output discharge. The SPI lines to the camera have series termination.

![Camera load switch and SPI connector schematic](/assets/images/hab-hat-sch-camera.png)

# Key features
- **Ideal-diode 5 V input.** An LM74700-Q1 drives a 7.5 mΩ AON7400A MOSFET.
  - It feeds the Pi through header pins 2/4 with about a 20 mV forward drop.
  - It blocks back-feed from a USB supply plugged into the Pi, and reverse-polarity input.
  - The path is sized for 2.5 A continuous.
- **Switched camera power.** A TPS22918 load switch (about 0.7 ms rise time, quick output discharge) is enabled from a GPIO with a pull-down, so the camera is off at boot.
- **Six Qwiic / STEMMA QT I2C ports** (JST-SH), all in parallel.
  - ESD diodes protect the port whose sensor cable exits the capsule.
  - One port's 3.3 V pin goes through an open-by-default solder jumper, so a module with its own supply can't be tied to the Pi's 3.3 V rail.
- **GNSS port** (JST-GH 5-pin) with UART and a PPS input, labelled from the Pi's side.
- **SPI camera port** (JST-GH 6-pin):
  - 33 Ω series resistors at the Pi header;
  - a 0 Ω MISO footprint at the connector;
  - routing over a continuous ground plane, for 8 MHz over a 10–20 cm harness.
- **Raspberry Pi HAT ID EEPROM** (24LC32).
  - 3.9 kΩ pull-ups on the ID bus.
  - Write-protected by default.
  - A test pad and a jumper footprint allow reprogramming.
- **Locking 5 V input connector:** JST-VH through-hole, side entry.
- **No always-on LEDs.** The optional indicator is behind an open solder jumper.

# Electrical / mechanical notes
- **Edge budget:**
  - Every connector had to sit on a board edge, clear of the Pi's mounting-screw heads and header.
  - The first edge-fit study found 87 mm of connector width needed, against about 86 mm available.
  - One spare I2C port was dropped to fit.
- **SPI without vias:**
  - The Pi's pin order and the camera connector's pin order force three signal crossings.
  - Instead of hopping to the bottom layer, which would slot the ground plane under the SPI traces, each crossing runs underneath an 0603 series resistor.
  - As a result, the SPI stays on the top layer over an unbroken ground plane.
- **Power routing:**
  - The 5 V path uses a 1.0–1.2 mm trunk, about an 11 °C rise at 2.5 A (IPC-2221).
  - Both layers carry ground pours, with automatically placed stitching vias.
- **Stack height:** 2.5 mm Pi header plus an 8.5 mm socket gives an 11 mm gap, set with M2.5 × 11 mm nylon standoffs.
- **Temperature:** everything is rated −40 °C or better except the JST connectors (−25 °C).
- **Design flow:** KiCad 10.
  - The schematic was written as hierarchical sheets, and the layout was hand-routed.
  - Placement, routing and the fab outputs are generated by Python scripts, so the board regenerates reproducibly.
  - Verification: ERC 0, DRC 0, schematic-to-PCB parity 0, and a script that checks all 100 header and connector pins against the pin allocation.

# Fabrication and test
- **Fab package:** gerbers, drill files, BOM and pick-and-place files for JLCPCB assembly; assembly drawings; STEP model; and a mating-cable list for the harnesses.
- **Bench acceptance checks:**
  - no shorts between rails;
  - the Pi boots from the HAT input with `get_throttled` reporting 0x0;
  - every I2C device enumerates;
  - the EEPROM reads back;
  - GPIO toggles the camera rail;
  - there is no back-feed with USB power applied to the Pi.

# Use cases
- Flight computer for high-altitude balloon payloads: sensors, GNSS, camera and telemetry.
- A general Pi Zero sensor-hub HAT for tidy, keyed wiring in harsh or mobile environments.
- A reference for ideal-diode power input and switched peripheral rails on Pi HATs.
